---
title: I redesigned my portfolio by deleting things
description: Fewer sections, no fake skill percentages, and about 15,000 fewer lines.
date: 2026-10-23
---

My old portfolio had everything. A navbar. Fixed social bars on both sides. Count-up stats. Skill circles telling you I was 87% JavaScript. A contact form. Gradient blobs floating in the background.

The new one is a single column. My name, a few sentences, my projects, my writing. That's it.

## Skill percentages mean nothing

What does "85% Next.js" even mean? 85% of what? Nobody reads it and thinks "great, let's hire them." It's decoration pretending to be data.

I deleted all of it. If you want to know what I can build, look at what I built.

## Counters are noise

"30 projects completed. 350 commits." Animated numbers that count up when you scroll. They feel impressive for about one second and say nothing after that.

Gone.

## The diff was mostly red

The redesign removed around 15,000 lines, most of them from dependencies I didn't need anymore: animation libraries, a CMS I wasn't using, styling libraries I'd stopped touching. The page got simpler and faster at the same time.

## What I kept

- One font for reading, one serif for a single accent
- Plain links instead of buttons
- A list of projects you can actually scan
- Dark mode, because it's free when your colors are variables

Minimal design isn't about looking clean. It's about removing everything that doesn't help someone understand who you are in ten seconds.
