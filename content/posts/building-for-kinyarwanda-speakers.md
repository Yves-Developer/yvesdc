Most of the tools I use every day assume English. Some of my side projects don't get to.

A few of them are built for Kinyarwanda speakers: people who watch, search and learn in Kinyarwanda first.

That changes the work in ways you don't notice until you're in it.

## The defaults don't fit

Fonts, date formats, payment providers, voice models, even the copy a designer drops in as placeholder. Everything comes tuned for someone else.

So a lot of the job is noticing defaults and asking whether they make sense for the person on the other side of the screen. Usually they don't, quite.

## Voice is the hard part

I've been testing Kinyarwanda text-to-speech. The surprising part: the model says every word well. What it gets wrong is the pausing. Where a sentence breathes, where it stops.

That's a good lesson in general. Being technically correct, word by word, isn't the same as sounding right. Users feel the rhythm before they notice the words.

## Small market, real problems

It's tempting to build only for the biggest market. But a smaller audience that nobody is building for is a better place to be useful. People notice when something is made for them.

## What I've learned

- Test with real speakers early. They hear problems you can't.
- Don't translate the interface last. Write it in the language from the start.
- Local payment methods aren't a nice-to-have. They're the product.

Building for people who are usually an afterthought is some of the most satisfying work I do.
